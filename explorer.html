          How to read these terms

          Fair value is the fund’s quarter-end valuation. Amortized cost is its accounting cost basis. Principal is the face amount of the instrument and can be denominated in a different currency.

          First-lien debt has a senior claim on pledged collateral. Second-lien debt ranks behind the first lien. A floating coupon adds a spread to a benchmark. PIK interest accrues to principal. See the filing for instrument-specific terms.

          Remaining time runs from the reporting month to the stated maturity month. Original term requires an issue date, which this schedule does not give for the individual investments. Repayment, refinancing or changes to the agreement can affect timing.

          Portfolio totals and timing

          The 493 named position lines total $35.131 billion of fair value. The filing separately reports a negative $16.524 million valuation adjustment on unfunded commitments, bringing reported total investment fair value to $35.114 billion. All portfolio-share percentages use that reported total. Industry and issuer subtotals in the position view use the named positions before adjustments.

          The fund describes typical debt investment terms of three to ten years. Individual original terms require issue dates. The schedule provides maturity months, which let you calculate time remaining at September 2026. These are borrower deadlines. Separate fund terms govern shareholder liquidity.

          When the debt matures

          Compare the fair value of debt positions by scheduled maturity. Select a period to see the investments it contains.

          How the maturity breakdown works

          Each debt position is grouped by its contractual maturity month. Percentages divide the fair value in each period by total signed debt fair value in the selected scope. Equity and joint venture interests are excluded. A loan may repay early, be amended or default, so the dates do not predict cash receipts.

          What could still be drawn?

          At quarter end, OCIC had committed another $4.873 billion that portfolio companies could draw under their agreements. This included $2.220 billion of delayed-draw loans, $2.141 billion of revolving loans and $512.449 million of equity commitments.

          An expiry marks the end of the window to draw funds. Actual draws depend on the agreement and the company’s needs. The balances below measure additional potential funding.
